The Place

270 hectares,
one valley,
eight centuries.

The Monchique mountains hold this valley in a particular stillness. Almond trees planted during the Moorish occupation still fruit each winter. The limestone beneath the fairways is the same stone cut into arches above the villa courtyards.

Silves, the once-capital of the Moorish Algarve, is twelve minutes east. Armação de Pêra beach is seven minutes south. Between them: this resort, rooted where the hinterland decides to pause before reaching the sea.

The Faldo Course sits at the intersection of scrubland and precision. Walk the 12th at dusk when the limestone turns amber and understand why this place won Portugal's best course — not despite its austerity, but because of it.
