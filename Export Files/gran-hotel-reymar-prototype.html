Tossa de Mar

A coast that
resists erasure

The Costa Brava breaks into the Mediterranean in hard limestone folds here. Tossa de Mar has held its medieval walls since the 12th century — the sea has carved the cliffs since long before that. Time moves differently at this latitude.

The Tramuntana brings cold clarity from the north; the Garbí carries warm salt from the south. Between them, Posidonia meadows breathe beneath the surface, filtering light into aquamarine that has no adequate name in any language.

Gran Hotel Reymar occupies the headland above it all — close enough to feel the spray on still mornings, high enough to watch the horizon redden at dusk without obstruction.
