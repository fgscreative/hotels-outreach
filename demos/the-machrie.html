The Place

Islay. The Queen of the Hebrides.

Just off Scotland's Atlantic coast — a short flight from Glasgow — Islay holds more in its 240 square miles than most places manage in a continent. Wind-scoured machair. Revetted links bunkers. The smell of peat smoke meeting salt air at the dune deck.

The landscape here is not scenery. It is the reason the whisky tastes the way it does. The reason the links plays the way it does. The reason guests return, often within the year.

Forty-three rooms, all facing the view. No room turns its back on the island.
