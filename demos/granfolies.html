The Place

Port d’Andratx,
Mallorca

The cliffs at Cala Llamp drop fourteen metres into water the colour of pale glass. This particular rock has been a shelter, a fishing ground, and since 1999, the ground beneath Gran Folies — a place where people slow their pulse and eat well with the open sea at their table.

Port d’Andratx sits at Mallorca’s southwestern limit — further from the island’s interior than from the Atlas Mountains on a clear day. The light here has its own quality: dense at dawn, silver at noon, amber through every gradation until the rock goes dark.

The Mediterranean here holds 28°C deep into September. The Posidonia oceanica meadows below are among the oldest living organisms on earth. You swim directly above them.
