The Place

Cogne.
At the foot
of everything.

Cogne sits at the confluence of four valleys, sealed on three sides by permanent snowpack. The Gran Paradiso massif rises directly above — at 4,061 metres, the only major peak lying entirely on Italian territory.

This is not a retreat from the world. It is a return to a version of it that most people have forgotten. The Dora di Cogne river runs below our terrace. In June, the meadows hold gentian and wild thyme. In February, silence has weight.

Italy's oldest national park was born here in 1922 to protect the ibex from extinction. The ecosystem it shelters — glacier, forest, high pasture, rock wall — forms the backdrop against which everything at Bellevue is measured.
