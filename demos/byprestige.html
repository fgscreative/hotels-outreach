The Place

Scottish Borders.

The Scottish Borders does not announce itself. It unfolds — through a gap in the treeline, across a river bend, along the shadow of a 16th-century wall. This land has been shaped by centuries of deliberate living.

At 220 metres above sea level, the air at Barony Castle carries a clarity that cannot be manufactured. The estate sits within 25 acres of grounds where the landscape has been tended, not staged — a distinction felt on the first morning walk.

Eddleston Water flows 800 metres north. The Pentland Hills form the horizon. The Tweed Valley lies beyond. This is not a setting chosen for scenery. It works on the body in ways that resist simple explanation.
