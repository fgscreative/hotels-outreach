The Place

Clichy,
between two
versions of Paris.

Clichy sits at latitude 48.9016, where the Seine bends north and the old factories still leave their geometry on the skyline. Seventeen minutes on foot from Montmartre. A neighbourhood that carries both the weight of its industrial past and the ease of a city that has learned to breathe again.

The hotel occupies a block on Rue Villeneuve, shaped by the Argentine architect Paula Chauvigné. She kept the bones: steel columns, raw concrete, high light. Then brought in cedar, Carrara marble, hand-hammered brass — materials that cool in the morning and hold warmth at night.

Your room tonight might be Helsinki. Amsterdam tomorrow. The city outside never changes. What changes is how you see it — each morning, a different lens.
