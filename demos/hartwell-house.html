The Place

A Vale
That Held Kings
and Kept Quiet

Hartwell House sits within the Vale of Aylesbury at the precise point where the lowland gives way to the rising chalk of the Chilterns. The estate was recorded in Domesday, shaped over centuries by men of the law, and leased to Louis XVIII of France in his exile — the French court operated here for five years before Napoleon fell.

The gardens were designed by Richard Woods, a contemporary of Capability Brown. The bones of that landscape — the lake, the serpentine paths, the framed views — remain intact. The National Trust holds the freehold. Very little changes, and that is the point.

Forty miles from London. A different century entirely.
