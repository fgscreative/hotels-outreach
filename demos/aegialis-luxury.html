Amorgos · Cyclades · Aegean

The island that
resisted being found

Amorgos is the easternmost inhabited island of the Cyclades, separated from Santorini by open Aegean. There is no airport. The ferry takes five hours from Piraeus. That inaccessibility is not a limitation — it is the first act of the island's curation.

The Aigiali valley is a rare geological pocket: terraced hillsides of phrygana scrub and endemic Cycladic medicinal herbs descend to a bay of extraordinary clarity. Below the surface, Posidonia oceanica meadows filter the water to a transparency the eye reads as depth.

The Monastery of Panagia Chozoviotissa — carved into a 300-metre cliff face in 1088 AD — has been continuously inhabited for nine centuries. The island doesn't perform its history. It simply continues it.
