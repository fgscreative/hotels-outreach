At the southwestern tip of Cyprus, where limestone cliffs fall toward turquoise Mediterranean water, Aphrodite Hills occupies terrain considered sacred for three millennia. The ancient Sanctuary of Aphrodite at nearby Kouklia drew pilgrims from across the known world — a fact the landscape still communicates on clear mornings.

Petra tou Romiou — the rock formation where mythology holds that Aphrodite rose from the sea foam — lies four kilometres down the coast. From the resort's clifftop trails, it is visible at dawn. The light that falls on that water at first hour has not changed since Homer described it.

The estate spans 578 acres of indigenous Cypriot habitat: carob trees, ancient olive groves, juniper, and the natural gorges that shape the golf course's most dramatic turns.
