Castelldefels · Garraf Coast · Barcelona

Twenty-five kilometres from the city.
A world away from it.

Castelldefels lies on the southern arc of the Barcelona metropolitan coast, where the Garraf Natural Park pushes limestone cliffs to within metres of the sea. The five-kilometre beach is oriented southwest, which means afternoon light across the full length of it until the sun drops behind the massif.

The town sits at sea level. Behind it, the Garraf plateau rises to 594 metres in under two kilometres. The contrast between beach and mountain here is not scenic — it is geological. The same limestone that built the Sagrada Família defines this coastline.

A direct Rodalies train connects Castelldefels to Barcelona Passeig de Gràcia in 35 minutes. The airport is 12 kilometres north. Both facts are useful once, then irrelevant — which is precisely the point of arriving here.
